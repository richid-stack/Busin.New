---
name: status-update
description: Write a short progress report or weekly update for a manager, client or team from rough notes. Use when the user asks for a status update, progress report, weekly report, stand-up note or project update.
---

Goal: the reader sees in 30 seconds whether things are on track and what they need to do.

**Steps**
1. Collect from the user, or from what they pasted: what was finished, what is in progress, what is blocked or late, what comes next, and any decision or help needed. Ask only for what is missing and cannot be guessed.
2. Lead with the overall state in one line: on track, at risk or blocked, and why in a few words. Be honest: a green status that hides a late deliverable helps nobody.
3. Group the rest into done, next and risks. Put numbers on things where they exist (3 of 5 pages done, 12 days late).
4. Make asks explicit: who needs to decide or do what, by when.

**Output**
- **Status**: one line.
- **Done since last update**: short bullets, results not activity ("Signed off the logo", not "Worked on the logo").
- **Next**: what happens before the next update.
- **Risks and blockers**: each with what is needed to clear it.
- **Needs from you**: decisions or help, with a date.
- Keep to one screen. For a client, drop internal detail and jargon.

**Rules**
- Do not invent progress, dates or percentages. If something is unknown, write "to be confirmed".
- Do not hide a problem in the middle of a long list; put it where it is seen.
- If the user gives nothing to report yet, ask for their notes instead of writing filler.
