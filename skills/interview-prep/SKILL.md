---
name: interview-prep
description: Prepare someone for a job interview or a hard conversation, with likely questions, strong honest answers, questions to ask, and a practice round. Use when the user has an interview, asks how to answer a question, or wants to practise.
---

Goal: the person walks in knowing their best true stories and how to tell them in two minutes.

**Steps**
1. Learn the situation: the role and employer (use `web_search` and `web_fetch` for what the employer does and recent news), the stage (screening, panel, technical), and the user's background. Ask for their CV or a short history if you do not have it.
2. List the likely questions in groups: about them, about the role, behavioural ("tell me about a time..."), and the hard ones (gaps, leaving a job, salary, weaknesses).
3. Help them build five or six real stories from their own experience that can answer many questions. Shape each as situation, what they did, the result, and what they learned. Ask the user for the facts; never invent a story.
4. For each likely question, draft a short answer outline using their stories, not a script to memorise.
5. Prepare three good questions for the user to ask, specific to this employer and role.
6. Offer a practice round: ask one question at a time, wait for the answer, then give brief feedback on clarity, length, evidence and tone before the next one.

**Output**
- A one-page prep sheet: employer notes, likely questions, the stories, the questions to ask.
- During practice: one question at a time, feedback in three lines or fewer.

**Rules**
- Only true stories. If the user lacks experience for a question, help them answer honestly with related experience.
- On salary, give a way to research a range and to answer; do not state a market figure from memory, look it up and cite the source.
- Keep it encouraging and specific, not generic.
