---
name: proofread-and-tighten
description: Fix errors and make writing shorter and clearer while keeping the author's voice. Use when the user pastes text and asks to proofread, edit, improve, shorten, polish or make it sound better.
---

Goal: the same message, in the author's own voice, with the mistakes gone and the waste cut.

**Steps**
1. Check what the user wants: only fix errors, tighten, or change tone for a reader. If they did not say, fix errors and tighten, and keep the voice.
2. Read it all once before changing anything, so you know what the piece is for and who reads it.
3. Fix spelling, grammar, punctuation and wrong or missing words. Use the spelling the text already uses (British or American) and stay consistent.
4. Tighten: cut filler ("in order to", "it is important to note that"), repeated ideas, and weak openings. Prefer the concrete word to the vague one and active to passive where it reads better. Keep every fact, number, name and quote exactly as written.
5. Keep what makes it sound like them: their rhythm, their slang, their warmth. Do not make everything formal.

**Output**
- The edited text first, ready to copy.
- Then up to 5 bullets on the most useful changes, for example "Cut the second paragraph: it repeated the first". If the changes are small, say so in one line instead.
- If something looks wrong in substance (a number that does not add up, a claim that contradicts another), point it out separately; do not silently change it.

**Rules**
- Do not add new claims, examples or sections unless asked.
- If a sentence is ambiguous, ask or offer two readings; do not choose silently.
- For a short piece (a few lines), do not write a long explanation.
- Never invent facts, numbers or quotes while editing. If something is missing, ask.
