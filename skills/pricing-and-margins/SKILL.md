---
name: pricing-and-margins
description: Work out a sensible selling price, margin or break-even point for a product or service, including the costs people forget. Use when the user asks how much to charge, whether they are making money on something, or what they need to sell to cover costs.
---

Goal: a price that covers real costs and leaves the profit the owner wants.

**Steps**
1. Gather the costs for one unit: materials or goods, packaging, transport, mobile money or card fees, any levy, and the time or labour. Then the monthly fixed costs and the volume expected.
2. Use `calculator` for every step: cost per unit, price from a target margin, margin from a price, and break-even units as fixed costs divided by the profit per unit.
3. Show the difference between margin (profit as a share of the price) and markup (profit as a share of the cost). Many people mix them up.
4. If the user wants market context, use `web_search` to see what similar sellers charge, and name where you looked.
5. Give three options: a lower price, a middle price and a higher price, each with the profit per unit and the break-even volume.

**Output**
- A small table of the three prices with margin, profit per unit and break-even.
- A recommendation with one sentence of reasoning.
- The costs most likely to be missing.

**Rules**
- Show your arithmetic. Never state a margin without the numbers behind it.
- If a figure is an assumption, say so.
