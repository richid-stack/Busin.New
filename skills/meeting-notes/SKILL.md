---
name: meeting-notes
description: Turn a meeting transcript, call notes or a messy brain dump into a clean summary with decisions, action items, owners and open questions. Use when the user pastes notes or a transcript, or asks for meeting minutes or a follow-up.
---

Goal: someone who missed the meeting can read this in a minute and know what was decided and what they must do.

**Steps**
1. Read everything the user gave you. If there is nothing to summarize yet, ask them to paste the notes or transcript.
2. Separate four things that people often blur together: what was discussed, what was decided, what someone must do, and what stayed unresolved.
3. For every action item, find the owner and the due date in the text. If the owner or date was not said, write "owner not stated" or "no date given". Never assign people or invent deadlines.
4. If the user wants a follow-up email, draft it after the summary, in a short and friendly tone.

**Output**
- **Summary** in 2 to 4 sentences: the purpose of the meeting and the outcome.
- **Decisions**: a bullet list. Only things that were actually agreed.
- **Action items**: one line each as "Owner: task (due date)".
- **Open questions**: things raised but not settled.
- **Key points discussed**: at most 5 short bullets, only if they add context not covered above.
- **Follow-up email** only when asked.

**Rules**
- Stay faithful to the text. If something is ambiguous, flag it in one line instead of guessing.
- Keep names and numbers exactly as written.
- Leave out small talk and repetition.
- If notes are sensitive (salaries, health, legal issues), do not add commentary and do not share them anywhere.
