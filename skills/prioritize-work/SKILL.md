---
name: prioritize-work
description: Sort a messy list of tasks, requests or ideas into a clear order using impact, effort, urgency and deadlines. Use when the user feels overloaded, asks what to do first, or has many tasks or feature ideas to rank.
---

Goal: a short list of what to do now, what to schedule, and what to drop, with the reasons.

**Steps**
1. Collect the list as it is. Ask for the deadline of each, and what the user is trying to achieve this week or quarter. Without a goal, you cannot rank.
2. Mark each item: does it have a real deadline, who is waiting on it, how big is the effort (hours, days, weeks), and what happens if it is not done.
3. Choose a method that fits. For personal tasks: urgent and important first, then important but not urgent (schedule it), then urgent but not important (delegate or shorten), and the rest (drop or park). For product or project ideas: score reach, impact, confidence and effort, and compute reach times impact times confidence divided by effort with `calculator`.
4. Look for quick wins (high value, under an hour), tasks blocked on someone else (send the nudge today), and tasks that should be split.
5. Name what to stop: things that do not serve the goal. Say it plainly.

**Output**
- **Do now** (the top 3), **Schedule** (with suggested days), **Delegate or shorten**, **Drop or park**, each with a one-line reason.
- A suggested order for the next day or week if wanted.

**Rules**
- The user owns the goal; if two priorities conflict, ask which wins.
- Do not pretend scores are exact. Show the reasoning so they can change a number.
- Be honest about capacity: if the list cannot fit in the time, say so.
- Never invent deadlines, effort or impact. Use what the user said, or ask.
